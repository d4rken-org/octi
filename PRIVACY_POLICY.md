# Privacy policy

This is the privacy policy for the Android app **Octi - Multi-Device Sync** (package
`eu.darken.octi`), written and published by Matthias Urhahn (darken).

*Last updated: 22 September 2026*

## Summary

Octi lets you check your other Android devices' battery, network, clipboard and installed apps, and
send files between them. You choose how the devices exchange this data.

* Octi requires no sign-up, password or email address. An Octi Server account is a random
  identifier created by the app, not a profile about you.
* Octi contains no ads, no analytics SDK and no tracking of any kind.
* I do not collect, sell, rent or share your personal information.
* Your device data leaves your device over the sync service you choose, and only so that your own
  other devices can read it. The one request that goes elsewhere is the public IP lookup described
  below.

If something here is unclear, send a [quick mail](mailto:support@darken.eu) and I will answer it.
My underlying privacy principle is the [Golden Rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Rule).

## What Octi reads from your device

Octi is built from modules. Battery, connectivity, WiFi, installed apps, clipboard and file transfer
each have an on/off switch in the app's settings. Basic device info is always part of a sync,
because without it the other devices cannot tell which device they are looking at.

| Module | What it reads and syncs |
|---|---|
| Battery | Charge level, charging status, charging speed, charging current, battery health and temperature, and estimated time until full or empty |
| Connectivity | Connection type (WiFi, cellular, ethernet), local IPv4 and IPv6 address, gateway address (usually your router), DNS servers, and your public IP address |
| WiFi | Network name (SSID), signal strength, and whether the network is 2.4 GHz or 5 GHz |
| Clipboard | Only text you explicitly send with the share action or the clipboard widget. Octi does not watch your clipboard in the background. |
| Installed apps | For each installed app: package name (the app's identifier), app name, version, install and update time, and which app installed it |
| Device info | A device ID generated by Octi, a device name you can set yourself, manufacturer and model, device type, Android version, API level and security patch date, last boot time, and the Octi version and build revision |
| File transfer | Only files you explicitly pick to send. For each one: file name, file type, size, a checksum, and when it was shared and expires |

This information is shown on your own devices, and nowhere else. It is not sent to me, and no part
of it is used to build a profile, to advertise, or to train anything.

Turning a module off stops it collecting and syncing new data. Transfers and deletions for files
already shared continue in the background until they finish.

## How your devices exchange this data

Octi shares data between your own devices, not with other users. Each device writes its data to
Octi Server or Google Drive for your other devices to read. You choose which service each device
uses.

### Octi Server

Octi Server is an open-source sync server. I run one, and you may also
[host your own](https://github.com/d4rken-org/octi-server).

Module data and transferred files are encrypted on your device with AES-256 authenticated encryption
before they are uploaded. The encryption key is generated on your device and travels between your
devices in the QR code you scan when linking them. It is never sent to the server, so the server,
and therefore I, cannot read the contents of your synced data.

The following is not encrypted, so the server can read it:

* Your account and device identifiers, which are random values generated by the app.
* Details sent with each request as HTTP headers: your Octi version, the platform (`android`), your
  device model, and which sync protocol features your version supports.
* Access times and IP addresses, stored temporarily to limit how often devices can make requests and
  to block abuse.

Creating an account requires no email address, no password and no personal details.

You can delete everything the server holds for you from inside the app, by deleting your account.
Unused data is also removed on its own: a device that has not contacted the server for 90 days is
deleted together with its synced data, and an account that is left with no devices is deleted too.

### Google Drive

If all your devices are signed in to the same Google account, Octi can use Google Drive instead of a
server. See [Google user data](#google-user-data) below for the full detail of what this involves.

Data stored this way is **not** end-to-end encrypted. It is held in Drive's hidden application data
folder, which only Octi can access, and it is protected by your Google account rather than by a key
only your devices know. If you sync over Google Drive, you and Google can access that data. I cannot.

### Public IP address lookup

The connectivity module shows your device's public IP address. A device cannot determine its own
public address by itself, so it asks an Octi Server. Any Octi Server you have configured is asked
first, and the default Octi Server that I run is always kept as a last fallback, including when you
have configured your own.

The server sees your public IP address when it answers the lookup. Nothing else is recorded for it.
Turning off the connectivity module stops these requests.

### On your own device

Octi keeps a copy of the synced state of your other devices in its private app cache, so the
dashboard still works while offline. This data is removed when you clear the app's cache or
uninstall it.

## Google user data

This section covers Octi's use of Google account data and the Google Drive API. It applies only if
you connect a Google account for Drive syncing. If you only use Octi Server, Octi requests no Google
data at all.

### Which permissions Octi requests, and why

| OAuth scope | What Octi does with it |
|---|---|
| `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.appdata` | Reads and writes the device state and transferred files your other devices need, inside Drive's hidden application data folder. This folder is created for Octi, is not visible in your Drive file list, and cannot be read by other apps. |
| `openid`, `email` | Reads the account ID and email address of the account you picked, so the app can label it in the account list and check that every device is linked to the same account. |

Octi requests no other scope. It cannot list, open, create or delete your ordinary Drive files,
documents, photos or folders, and it never asks you for that permission. The `drive.appdata` scope
grants access to Octi's own hidden folder only.

### What is stored, where, and for how long

* The **synced device state** and any **files you send** live in the application data folder of your
  own Google Drive. Device state stays until Octi replaces it with newer state. Shared files expire
  48 hours after you share them, and are cleaned up then or when you delete the share in the app.
* The **account ID and email address** are stored locally on your device, in Octi's private app
  storage, so that the app can show you which account it is connected to. They are not uploaded
  anywhere and are never sent to me. Syncing over Google Drive involves no Octi account and no
  server of mine. (The connectivity module separately asks an Octi Server for your public IP
  address, as described above, but that request carries no Google data.)
* **Access tokens** let Octi prove to Google that it has permission to use your account. Google Play
  services obtains and refreshes them on your device. Octi sends them only to Google's own APIs,
  does not save them itself, and never sends one to me or to any third party.

### Sharing

Google user data obtained through these scopes is not shared with anybody. It is not sent to me, to
an analytics provider, to an advertiser, or to any other third party. The only parties involved are
your devices and Google.

One exception is under your control: a [debug log](#debug-log) that you generate and share yourself
can contain your Google account ID and the email address of the connected account.

### Removing it

* **Reset**, on the Google Drive entry in Octi's sync settings, deletes the synced device state held
  in the application data folder, while leaving the device signed in. Files you transferred are
  stored separately and are not covered by it; delete those from the app's file list, or let them
  expire after 48 hours.
* **Disconnect**, on the same screen, signs this device out, deletes this device's synced state, and
  removes the locally stored account ID and email address.
* Disconnecting does not withdraw the permission you granted. Revoke Octi's access to your Google
  account at [your Google account's permission page](https://myaccount.google.com/permissions).
* Uninstalling Octi removes all data Octi held on that device.

### Limited Use

Octi's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app will adhere to the
[Google API Services User Data Policy](https://developers.google.com/terms/api-services-user-data-policy),
including the Limited Use requirements.

Octi does not use or transfer Google user data for advertising, sell it, or allow anyone other than
you to read it.

## Android permissions

### Location

Viewing information about your current WiFi network requires location permission
(`ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION` / `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION`) on newer Android versions. Android treats
nearby WiFi networks as location data, because knowing which networks are around you narrows down
where you are.

Granting it is optional. Without it, Octi simply does not show things like the WiFi network name.
Octi does not request GPS coordinates and does not track your location.

### Camera

The camera (`CAMERA`) is used to scan QR codes, which is how two devices are linked: one device
shows a QR code holding the account data, the other scans it. Camera frames are processed on the
device to find the code and are never stored or transmitted.

### Query installed apps

Showing which apps are installed on your other devices requires `QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES`.

This list is visible only to you, on your own devices. When you sync over Octi Server, it is
encrypted both during transfer and while stored. It can also appear in a [debug log](#debug-log)
that you choose to generate and share.

## Debug log

A debug log is a detailed record of what Octi is doing, used to investigate problems. Recording
starts only when you turn it on in the app's settings. You can share the log file through Android's
sharing menu, for example by emailing it to me.

This file can contain sensitive information, such as your list of installed apps, your WiFi network
name, or the Google account ID and email address of a connected Google account. Only share it with
people you trust.

## Children

Octi is not directed at children and does not knowingly collect information from them. The app asks
for no personal registration, and I hold no profile that could identify a user.
